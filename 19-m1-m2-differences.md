# Chapter 19 — Differences between M1 and M2


Summary: the behaviour of the M1 sequencer (S5L8702, iPod nano 3G) set against
the M2 sequencer (S5L8720, iPod nano 4G), first as a table of the substantive
differences by topic, then finding by finding for all 175 M1 findings. M2-nn
refers to [chapter 18](18-ledger.md); M1-Fnnn to the
[M1 ledger](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md). The same comparison in
machine-readable form, with the device experiments of section 19.4, is
`validation/m1-m2-crosswalk.json`.

Verdicts: **same** — M2 behaves as the M1 claim states, over the claim's whole
scope; **differs** — at least one part of the claim is different on M2 (both
sides are stated); **m2-only-context** — the claim concerns a nano 3G board, chip
or firmware detail, and the M2 cell gives the nano 4G counterpart; **unresolved**
— the M2 cell gives the measured state and states which part of the claim is not
measured on M2. "Source" marks a fact read from the original M2 programs or
CPU driver rather than measured on the device.

## 19.1 Substantive differences

### Instruction set

| On M1 | On M2 |
|---|---|
| `jr` (`0f`) jumps unconditionally to r[arg], unrounded; R ignored; a target above `0xfff` does not jump ([M1-F053](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md), [M1-F097](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | `jr` jumps to r[arg] only if r[R] ≠ 0; the low three target bits are ignored; no wrap up to 2^20, a target above `0xfff` jumps ([M2-110](18-ledger.md), [M2-122](18-ledger.md)) |
| `bz`/`bnz` take unaligned targets as given ([M1-F070](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | branch targets ignore their low three bits; PC ≥ 25 bits ([M2-105](18-ledger.md), [M2-725](18-ledger.md)) |
| source selector 10 is the destination in MOV/immediate forms, r0 in the register form ([M1-F069](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | source selector 10 is the destination in every MOV and ALU form, register form included ([M2-141](18-ledger.md)) |
| `08` costs the same as `movi` ([M1-F052](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | `08` costs 1.25 cycles (37.6 ns), `ff` and `movi` 1 cycle; `08` with R = 8..`0xff` stalls until the timeout fault; an all-ones word runs as `ff`; every value `1a..fe` acts as an unmet wait also with a pending flag ([M2-102](18-ledger.md), [M2-378](18-ledger.md), [M2-625](18-ledger.md), [M2-367](18-ledger.md), [M2-583](18-ledger.md)) |
| `wait` ignores arg/imm ([M1-F070](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | `wait 12` uses arg and imm as status operand and mask; it passes iff status & arg & mask ≠ 0 ([M2-134](18-ledger.md)) |
| the original programs use `pld`/`pst` on `0xd60..0xd7c` ([M1-F001](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md), [M1-F003](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | no original program uses `pld`/`pst`; the engine executes them ([M2-08](18-ledger.md)) |
| `15` toggles `C14`: consecutive `tmwait`s alternate a full wait and an immediate reload ([M1-F047](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md), [M1-F171](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | each `tmwait` ends at the (count + 1)-th timeout instant after its entry (492.747 us × count + 450 us from start-word bits 15:8 when entered at the start), faults for count 0, ignores its fields and `C14` writes and leaves `C14` = 0; the next one waits again ([M2-373](18-ledger.md), [M2-584](18-ledger.md), [M2-629](18-ledger.md), [M2-150](18-ledger.md)) |
| `end` faults when R or arg is non-zero ([M1-F105](../../nano3g/FMSS_M1/18-ledger.md)) | `end` ignores non-zero R, arg and imm and latches its word and imm in `C40`/`C44` ([M2-164](18-ledger.md)) |
